Introduction

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyber attack where criminals pretend to be a trusted person or organization to trick you into revealing sensitive information such as passwords, bank details, or OTPs.

These attacks often arrive through emails, text messages, social media, or fake websites that look genuine.

Steals passwords and personal information
Uses fake emails, messages, or websites
Targets anyone connected to the Internet
Attack Process

How a Phishing Attack Works

Phishing attacks usually follow a predictable pattern. Understanding each step helps you recognize and stop the attack before becoming a victim.

Step 1

Fake Email or Message

The attacker sends an email or message pretending to be from a trusted company, bank, school, or friend.

Step 2

Victim Clicks the Link

The message creates urgency or fear, encouraging the victim to click a malicious link.

Step 3

Fake Website Opens

The victim is redirected to a fake website that closely resembles the real one.

Step 4

Credentials Are Entered

The victim enters their username, password, OTP, or banking details, believing the website is legitimate.

Step 5

Attacker Steals Information

The attacker receives the stolen information and may use it to access accounts, steal money, or commit identity fraud.

Remember: Most phishing attacks succeed because they trick people into acting quickly. Always pause, verify the sender, and think before clicking any link.

Different Types

Common Types of Phishing

Phishing attacks come in many forms. Knowing the different types helps you recognize scams before they can harm you.

Email Phishing

Fake emails pretending to be from trusted companies asking you to click links or provide sensitive information.

Smishing

Phishing through SMS or text messages containing fake links, OTP requests, or prize scams.

Vishing

Attackers call victims pretending to be bank officials or technical support to steal confidential information.

Spear Phishing

A highly targeted phishing attack aimed at a specific person or organization using personalized information.

Whaling

A phishing attack that targets executives, CEOs, or senior managers to steal valuable business information.

Social Media Phishing

Fake profiles and messages on social media that trick users into revealing passwords or clicking malicious links.

Spot the Scam

How to Identify a Phishing Email

Phishing emails often look convincing, but they usually contain warning signs. Learn to identify these red flags before clicking any links.

From: support@amaz0n-security.com Subject: ⚠️ Your Account Will Be Suspended Today!

Hello Customer,

We detected unusual activity on your account. To avoid permanent suspension, verify your account immediately by clicking the button below.

Verify Account

Failure to verify within 24 hours will permanently disable your account.

Thank you,
Amazon Security Team

Fake Email Address

Notice the sender uses amaz0n with a zero instead of the official domain.

Creates Urgency

Attackers pressure victims to act immediately without thinking.

Suspicious Link

Always hover over links before clicking to check where they actually lead.

Generic Greeting

"Hello Customer" instead of your real name is a common phishing sign.

Warning Signs

Red Flags to Watch For

Most phishing attacks contain warning signs. If you notice one or more of these red flags, stop and verify before taking any action.

Unknown Sender

The email comes from an unfamiliar or suspicious address that doesn't match the official company domain.

Urgent Messages

Messages saying "Act now!", "Your account will be blocked!", or "Limited time!" are common phishing tactics.

Suspicious Links

Always check links carefully before clicking. Attackers often hide fake websites behind shortened or misleading URLs.

Unexpected Attachments

Don't open attachments you weren't expecting, especially ZIP, EXE, or unknown file types.

Poor Grammar

Many phishing emails contain spelling mistakes, awkward wording, or unusual formatting.

Requests for Sensitive Information

Legitimate companies rarely ask for passwords, OTPs, or banking details through email or messages.

Remember

If an email or message makes you feel pressured, promises unrealistic rewards, or asks for personal information, treat it as suspicious until you verify it.

Smarter Security

Passphrases & Password Managers

Remembering dozens of strong passwords can be difficult. Passphrases and password managers make staying secure much easier.

Passphrase

A passphrase is a long, memorable combination of words that is much harder to crack than a short password.

Example Coffee&Sunrise2026!
  • Easy to remember
  • Harder to guess
  • More secure than short passwords

Password Manager

A password manager securely stores your passwords and can generate unique, strong passwords for every account.

Benefits

Store • Generate • Autofill

  • One master password
  • Unique password for every account
  • No need to remember dozens of passwords

Best Practice: Use a memorable passphrase for your password manager's master password, then let the manager generate strong, unique passwords for all your other accounts.

Stay Safe

What to Do If You Receive a Phishing Email

If you suspect an email or message is a phishing attempt, follow these steps to protect yourself and your personal information.

01

Don't Click Anything

Avoid clicking links, downloading attachments, or replying to suspicious messages.

02

Verify the Sender

Check the sender's email address and confirm the message through the company's official website or phone number.

03

Delete or Report

Mark the email as phishing or spam, then delete it from your inbox.

04

Change Your Password

If you accidentally entered your password, change it immediately and update any reused passwords.

05

Enable MFA

Turn on Multi-Factor Authentication to add an extra layer of protection to your accounts.

06

Contact the Organization

If you're unsure whether the message is genuine, contact the organization using its official website or customer support number.

Golden Rule

Stop • Think • Verify. Taking a few extra seconds to check a suspicious message can prevent identity theft, financial loss, and account compromise.

Did You Know?

Interesting Phishing Facts

01

Anyone Can Be Targeted

Phishing attacks target students, employees, businesses, and everyday Internet users—not just large companies.

02

Scammers Copy Real Brands

Attackers often imitate banks, shopping websites, delivery services, and social media platforms.

03

Mobile Users Are Targeted Too

Phishing is not limited to emails. Fake SMS messages and messaging apps are also commonly used.

04

Awareness Is Your Best Defense

Learning to recognize phishing attempts greatly reduces the chance of becoming a victim.

Common Mistakes

Avoid These Phishing Mistakes

Clicking Without Checking

Always inspect links before clicking them.

Panicking

Scammers create urgency to make victims act without thinking.

Sharing Passwords or OTPs

Legitimate organizations never ask for your password or OTP through email or messages.

Opening Unknown Attachments

Unexpected attachments may install malware on your device.

Ignoring Browser Warnings

Security warnings are designed to protect you from dangerous websites.

Trusting Every Email

Always verify the sender before responding or taking action.

Real-Life Examples

Common Phishing Scenarios

Fake Bank Alert

"Your account has been locked. Click here to verify your identity."

Delivery Scam

"Your package is waiting. Pay a small fee to complete delivery."

Shopping Website

"Your order has a problem. Sign in immediately to avoid cancellation."

SMS Scam

"Congratulations! You've won a prize. Click here to claim it."

Fake Technical Support

"Your computer has a virus. Call this number immediately."

Social Media Login

"Your account violated our policy. Log in to restore access."

Quick Recap

What You Learned

  • Phishing is a cyber attack that tricks people into revealing sensitive information.
  • Always verify the sender before clicking links or opening attachments.
  • Look for warning signs such as urgency, fake domains, and suspicious links.
  • Never share passwords, OTPs, or banking details through emails or messages.
  • If you suspect phishing, report it, delete it, and change your password if necessary.
  • Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) to add extra protection.
