Quick Recap
Introduction

What is Network Security?

Network Security is the practice of protecting computers, devices, and data while they communicate over the Internet or local networks.

It prevents unauthorized access, protects sensitive information, and keeps online communication secure.

Protects personal information
Prevents hackers from accessing networks
Keeps online communication secure
First Line of Defense

What is a Firewall?

A firewall acts like a security guard between your device and the internet. It monitors incoming and outgoing network traffic and blocks suspicious connections.

Filters Traffic

Checks all incoming and outgoing data before allowing it to pass through.

Blocks Attackers

Prevents unauthorized users and malicious software from accessing your device.

Applies Security Rules

Uses predefined rules to decide which traffic is safe and which should be blocked.

Tip: Never disable your firewall unless you fully understand the risks.

Privacy Protection

What is a VPN?

A Virtual Private Network (VPN) creates a secure, encrypted connection between your device and the internet, helping protect your privacy and data.

Encrypts Your Data

A VPN encrypts your internet traffic so others cannot easily read or steal your information.

Protects Your Privacy

It helps hide your IP address, making your online activity more private.

Safer Public Wi-Fi

Using a VPN on public Wi-Fi reduces the risk of attackers intercepting your data.

Secure Connection

Your data travels through a protected tunnel between your device and the VPN server.

Remember

A VPN improves privacy and security, but it does not replace antivirus software or safe browsing habits.

Home Network Security

How to Keep Your Wi-Fi Secure

Your Wi-Fi network is the gateway to all your connected devices. Securing it helps prevent unauthorized access and protects your personal information.

Use a Strong Password

Create a long password with uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and special characters.

Use WPA3 or WPA2

Enable WPA3 encryption if available. If not, use WPA2 to keep your Wi-Fi protected.

Change Default Router Password

Replace the router's default administrator password with a unique and secure one.

Update Router Firmware

Install firmware updates regularly to fix security issues and improve performance.

Disable WPS

Turn off Wi-Fi Protected Setup (WPS) because it can be exploited by attackers.

Create a Guest Network

Let visitors use a separate guest network instead of your main home network.

Quick Security Tip

Review your router's connected devices regularly. If you see an unknown device, change your Wi-Fi password immediately.

Stay Alert

Risks of Public Wi-Fi

Free Wi-Fi at airports, cafés, hotels, and shopping malls is convenient, but it can also expose your personal information if you're not careful.

Fake Wi-Fi Hotspots

Cybercriminals can create fake Wi-Fi networks with names similar to legitimate ones to trick users into connecting.

Data Interception

Attackers on the same network may attempt to intercept information if it isn't properly protected.

Financial Risk

Avoid online banking or entering payment details when using unsecured public Wi-Fi.

Malware Attacks

Poorly secured public networks can increase the risk of malware spreading between connected devices.

Account Theft

Logging into important accounts on unsafe networks may expose your credentials if additional protections are not in place.

Use a VPN

A VPN encrypts your connection, making it much harder for others on the network to view your data.

Best Practice

If you must use public Wi-Fi, avoid sensitive activities such as online banking or shopping, verify the network name with staff if possible, and use a trusted VPN whenever available.

Safe Browsing

Understanding HTTPS

Before entering passwords, payment details, or personal information on a website, make sure the connection is secure. HTTPS helps protect your data while it travels across the internet.

HTTP

HTTP sends data without encryption, making it easier for attackers to intercept information.

Less Secure

HTTPS

HTTPS encrypts the data exchanged between your browser and the website, helping protect sensitive information.

Secure

Look for the Padlock

A padlock icon in the address bar usually indicates that the website uses HTTPS.

Check the Website Address

Verify the website URL carefully to avoid fake websites that imitate trusted brands.

Shop Safely

Only enter payment information on trusted websites using HTTPS.

Important Note

HTTPS protects your connection, but it doesn't guarantee that a website is trustworthy. Always verify the website's identity before sharing sensitive information.

Best Practices

Keep Your Network Secure

Following a few simple habits can greatly reduce the risk of cyberattacks and help keep your devices and personal information safe.

Keep Devices Updated

Install operating system, browser, and router updates regularly to patch security vulnerabilities.

Enable Your Firewall

Leave your firewall enabled to help block unauthorized network traffic.

Use Strong Passwords

Protect your Wi-Fi router and online accounts with strong, unique passwords.

Use a VPN on Public Wi-Fi

A trusted VPN helps protect your internet traffic when using public wireless networks.

Download Carefully

Install software only from trusted sources and avoid unknown files or links.

Use HTTPS Websites

Before entering personal information, check that the website uses HTTPS.

Daily Network Security Checklist

  • Keep software and routers updated.
  • Use strong, unique passwords.
  • Enable your firewall.
  • Avoid suspicious websites and downloads.
  • Use HTTPS and VPN when appropriate.

Common Mistakes

Avoid These Network Security Mistakes

Using Public Wi-Fi Without Protection

Avoid accessing sensitive accounts on unsecured public networks.

Turning Off the Firewall

Disabling your firewall leaves your device more vulnerable.

Weak Wi-Fi Password

Simple passwords make it easier for attackers to access your network.

Ignoring Updates

Outdated devices and routers may contain known security flaws.

Ignoring HTTPS

Entering personal information on unsecured websites increases risk.

Downloading Unknown Files

Unknown downloads can introduce malware into your network.

Real-Life Examples

Everyday Network Security Situations

Café Wi-Fi

You use a VPN before checking your email on a coffee shop's Wi-Fi.

Airport Internet

You verify the official airport Wi-Fi before connecting.

Online Shopping

You check for HTTPS before entering payment details.

Home Wi-Fi

You change the default router password after installation.

Office Network

A firewall blocks unauthorized access attempts automatically.

Mobile Banking

You avoid banking on public Wi-Fi unless using a trusted VPN.
